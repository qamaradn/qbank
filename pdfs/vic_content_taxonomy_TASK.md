# TASK BRIEFING — VIC Selective Entry content taxonomy

> Written 2026-08-10. The Victorian equivalent of `nsw_content_taxonomy_TASK.md`.
> NSW went smoothly because its taxonomy was settled before a question was written.
> Nothing should be authored for VIC until this document is agreed.
>
> Every claim carries a confidence rating. Where a source is a coaching company rather
> than ACER, that is stated — those sites frequently describe the pre-2026 format.

---

## 1. THE EXAM

Sat in **Year 8** for **Year 9 entry**, at Melbourne High, Mac.Robertson Girls', Nossal
High and Suzanne Cory. The 2027-entry paper was sat **20 June 2026**; the 2028-entry
paper is expected mid-late June 2027.

ACER **restructured it for 2026**: five 30-minute components became three.

| # | Component | Time | Questions | Per question | Confidence |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | Mathematics and Quantitative Reasoning | 60 min | **~60–65** | ~55 s | Medium |
| 2 | Reading and Verbal Reasoning | 55 min | **75** | 44 s | High |
| 3 | Writing | 40 min | **2 tasks** | — | High |

Total task time 2 h 35 min; about four hours on site with breaks.

**Two corrections to `selective_exam_delivery_SPEC.md`:**

1. **Writing is TWO tasks, not one.** ACER states it plainly: *"The writing test consists
   of two tasks."* §3.2 of the delivery spec records one. This doubles the writing
   authoring requirement — see §5 below. **Confidence: High** (ACER's own site).
2. **Maths + Quantitative is ~60–65 questions, not 75.** §3.2.1 of the delivery spec
   estimated 75 by Method A (applying the Reading+Verbal question multiplier). Secondary
   sources describing the merged paper put maths at ~30–35 and quantitative at ~30, i.e.
   Method C — an administrative merge with little density change. **Confidence: Medium.**
   ACER does not publish the count and no first-hand student report of it was found.
   Planning figure revised to **62**.

### What ACER says each component assesses

Quoted from ACER's own preparation page — this is the only authoritative statement of
content that exists, and it is deliberately broad.

| Component | ACER's words |
|---|---|
| Mathematics | "year-level appropriate mathematical knowledge and reasoning, as well as the application of Mathematics to real-world contexts" |
| Quantitative Reasoning | "the ability to think and reason using numbers, patterns, and shapes, in both abstract and real-world contexts" |
| Reading | "the ability to access and retrieve information and to integrate, interpret, reflect on and evaluate ideas communicated in texts" |
| Verbal Reasoning | "the ability to think and reason using words, concepts and logic" |
| Writing | "the ability to convey ideas in precise language with ideas organised coherently" |

There is **no published subtopic breakdown and no official past paper.** Everything below
§2 is inferred from ACER's wording, the pre-2026 format, first-hand student accounts
recorded in `selective_verbal_reasoning_TASK.md`, and coaching-provider syllabuses.
Treat the subtopic *shares* as planning figures, not facts.

## 3. MATHEMATICS AND QUANTITATIVE REASONING — target 1,297

### 3.1 Subtopics and shares

Weightings from coaching-provider syllabuses, consistent across sources.
**Confidence: Medium.**

| Strand | Share | Subtopics |
|---|---:|---|
| **Number and Algebra** | **40–50%** | integers, fractions, decimals, percentages; order of operations; ratio and proportion; algebraic expressions (simplify, expand, factorise); linear equations and inequalities; formula substitution; arithmetic and geometric sequences; index laws; simultaneous equations; scientific notation |
| **Measurement and Geometry** | **25–35%** | perimeter, area, volume; angle rules (complementary, supplementary, vertically opposite, parallel lines); triangle, quadrilateral and circle properties; Pythagoras; coordinate geometry; transformations; composite shapes; similar and congruent figures; basic trigonometry |
| **Statistics and Probability** | **15–25%** | mean, median, mode, range; graph interpretation (bar, line, pie, histogram, stem-and-leaf); single and combined event probability; two-way tables and Venn diagrams; data collection and bias; misleading representations |

Quantitative Reasoning is not a separate content list — ACER describes it as *reasoning
with numbers, patterns and shapes*. In practice it is the same strands presented as
abstract puzzles rather than curriculum problems.

### 3.2 What we have, measured

`year9_maths` — **543 questions, the only maths pitched for VIC.** The NSW sets
(`year7_nsw_maths` 532, `ma_nsw_selective` 307) are Year 6–7 and are not transferable;
§4.1 of the delivery spec states the two-year gap is deliberate.

| Strand | Target | `year9_maths` | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Number and Algebra | 40–50% | 31% | **short** |
| Measurement and Geometry | 25–35% | 27% | OK |
| Statistics and Probability | 15–25% | **10%** | **short** |
| unclassified by topic label | — | 31% | needs relabelling before it can be judged |

`quantitative_reasoning` — **2,240 questions, pitch unverified.** 2,059 come from ACT
practice books whose briefings state `target_year: 11–12`; only 181 come from a Year 9–10
source.

### 3.3 THE DECISION THAT SIZES THIS COMPONENT

**Is the ACT-sourced quantitative bank usable at Year 8?** Nobody has read it.

- If **yes**: the component is oversupplied and needs only a Statistics top-up.
- If **no**: the gap is **~750 questions** and this becomes the largest job in the project.

Readability testing does not apply — quantitative items are short and numeric. This needs
a person to read **30 questions sampled across the ACT books** and answer one question:
*could a strong Year 8 student do this?*

**This is the single highest-value hour of work available.** Do it before anything else.

## 4. READING — target 743

### 4.1 Subtopics and shares

From ACER's wording — *access and retrieve, integrate, interpret, reflect on and
evaluate* — mapped onto the four skills that phrasing names. **Confidence: Low-Medium.**
ACER publishes no breakdown.

| # | Skill | Share | What it is |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 4.1 | Inference and implied meaning | **35%** | what is suggested, not stated |
| 4.2 | Author's purpose, tone and attitude | **25%** | what the writer is doing and why |
| 4.3 | Integrate and evaluate | **20%** | weigh a claim, compare parts of a text, judge evidence |
| 4.4 | Access and retrieve | **20%** | directly stated fact — ACER names it first, so it is real, but keep the share low |

**Text types:** narrative/literary, informational/expository, persuasive, and poetry.
Passages should run **150–200 words** — the paper allows 44 s per question, so long
passages do not fit.

### 4.2 What we have — the finding that changes the plan

**The 719 ACT-sourced reading questions are usable. This was checked, not assumed.**

An earlier assessment rejected them on a Flesch-Kincaid median of grade 16. That
assessment was wrong. FK is inflated by long-but-ordinary words — *acidification*,
*circumnavigating*, *pastoralists*, Australian place names. Three passages were read in
full at FK 12.5, 15.0 and 17.5; all three are comfortably within reach of a strong Year 8
selective candidate. They also run **161 words median**, which suits the 44-second pace
better than the NSW set does.

The NSW set (`rc_nsw_*`, 649) remains unusable for VIC — genuinely Year 6, FK median 6.2.

**But the ACT questions carry two exploitable defects:**

| Cheat strategy | Scores | Chance |
|---|---:|---:|
| Answer **C** to every question | **48%** | 25% |
| Pick the **longest option** every time | **51%** | 25% |

Key spread is A 4%, B 38%, **C 48%**, D 11%. The key averages 62 characters against 55
for distractors. **634 of these are approved and being served today** — this is a live
defect, not a VIC-only concern.

### 4.3 Verdict per work type

| Work | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Repair — key skew** | Shuffle options and update `correct_answer` on all 719. Mechanical; the `*_finalise` tools already do this for hand-built batches. **Only 13 explanations name an option position** and need a one-line edit first. |
| **Repair — length tell** | Pad short distractors so the key stops being the longest. Editing, not authoring, but a person must do it. |
| **Verify** | Read 20 questions and confirm they test inference rather than word-matching. ACT style leans on retrieval more than ACER's does; if the mix is retrieval-heavy, some rebalancing is needed. |
| **From scratch** | **~24 questions** to reach 743, plus whatever the verification pass rejects. |

**This takes VIC Reading from the largest gap in the project to a cleanup job.**

### 5.2 What we have

1,212 questions — the only VIC component with a raw surplus. But:

- **149 are banned types** (80 dictionary/alphabetical, 32 hidden words, 27 word codes,
  10 anagrams). **147 are approved and live.**
- **343 in total sit outside the taxonomy** — 28% of the bank.
- **The answer key is skewed** in the Gemini-generated books: `external_vr_300526` is
  **44% A**, `direct_ve_290526` is **43% B**. The hand-built `vr_vic_acer` is an even
  25/25/25/25.
- The mix is vocabulary-poor, which is the defect `selective_verbal_reasoning_TASK.md`
  §1 was written to fix and only partly did.

### 5.3 Verdict per work type

| Work | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Withdraw** | 149 banned-type questions. Blocked on the `qbank_id` fix — they cannot be reached in Selectly today. |
| **Repair — key skew** | Shuffle ~600 questions across the two skewed books. |
| **Review** | The remaining ~194 off-taxonomy questions are a judgement call: keep as drill-only filler, or withdraw. |
| **From scratch** | **~340** in the four short topics, weighted to vocabulary. |

## 6. WRITING — target 54

**This gap doubled on discovering the paper has two tasks.** 18 mock forms × 2 tasks = 36,
inflated 1.5× = **54 prompts**. We hold **10** `victorian_selective` prompts.

ACER assesses *"the ability to convey ideas in precise language with ideas organised
coherently"* and publishes no task-type list. The pre-2026 paper used one creative and one
analytical/persuasive task; two tasks in 40 minutes implies roughly 20 minutes each, so
each is short — a paragraph-to-page response, not the 300–400 words the NSW prompts assume.

**Confidence on task types: Low.** Worth a first-hand check before authoring 44 prompts.

| Work | Scope |
|---|---|
| **Recalibrate** | Existing 10 prompts were built to a 30-minute, single-task, 300–400 word assumption. Check they still fit a 20-minute task. |
| **From scratch** | **44 prompts**, split across the two task types once those are confirmed. |

## 7. THE WORK, IN ORDER

Nothing here is authoring. The first three items decide how big the authoring job is.

| # | Task | Effort | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Read 30 ACT quantitative questions.** Usable at Year 8? | 1 hour | Whether Maths+Quant needs 0 or ~750 questions |
| 2 | **Read 20 ACT reading questions.** Inference-led or retrieval-led? | 1 hour | Whether the 719 reuse holds |
| 3 | **Confirm the two VIC writing task types** from a first-hand account | — | The shape of 44 prompts |
| 4 | ~~Audit the answer-key skew across every subject~~ | **DONE — see §7.1** | It is bank-wide. Three subjects are exploitable |
| 5 | Agree the shares in §3.1, §4.1, §5.1 | — | The taxonomy itself |

Then, and only then:

| # | Task | Size |
|---|---|---:|
| 6 | Repair reading — shuffle keys, pad distractors, edit 13 explanations | 719 questions touched |
| 7 | Repair verbal — withdraw 149, shuffle ~600 | needs `qbank_id` first |
| 8 | Author verbal top-up, vocabulary-weighted | ~340 |
| 9 | Author writing prompts | 44 |
| 10 | Author maths Statistics & Probability top-up | ~100 |
| 11 | Author quantitative — **only if task 1 says the ACT bank fails** | 0 or ~750 |

**Best case ~484 new questions. Worst case ~1,234.** Task 1 is the difference.

### 7.1 The answer-key audit — RESULT

Run 2026-08-10 over every non-rejected question in the bank.

| Subject | n | A | B | C | D | Worst | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `logical_reasoning` | 954 | 25% | 25% | 25% | 25% | 25% | clean |
| `mathematics` | 1,380 | 25% | 25% | 25% | 25% | 25% | clean |
| `verbal_reasoning` | 1,203 | 28% | 30% | 24% | 18% | 30% | mild |
| `reading_comprehension` | 1,367 | 14% | 32% | **37%** | 17% | **37%** | **exploitable** |
| `quantitative_reasoning` | 2,195 | **38%** | 31% | 24% | 8% | **38%** | **exploitable** |
| `science_reasoning` | 1,317 | 16% | **43%** | 35% | 7% | **43%** | **exploitable** |

**About 4,900 questions carry an exploitable key skew, most of them approved and live.**
A student who answers B to every science question scores 43% without reading one of them.
In quantitative reasoning, D is the answer 8% of the time — a candidate who simply never
picks D loses almost nothing.

The two clean subjects are the two built by hand this year. Their finalisers shuffle
options for key balance; the Gemini pipeline never did. **This is a pipeline defect, not
a content defect** — no question is wrong, they are merely in the wrong order.

**It is therefore repairable without authoring.** Shuffle the options, update
`correct_answer`, skip the handful of explanations that name a position. The same repair
serves NSW, VIC and JMSS at once, and it is worth more per hour than any authoring
described in this document.

It is also the strongest argument yet for fixing `qbank_id` first: 4,900 live questions
cannot be corrected downstream until they can be addressed.
